# Phase 1B — Baseline First-Come-First-Served (FCFS) Simulation

## 1. Purpose of the Baseline

### 1.1 What This Model Measures

This document defines a traditional first-come-first-served onsale simulation that serves as the **control condition** for the allocation prototype. The FCFS baseline measures the outcomes that arise when inventory is distributed solely by arrival order — the earliest requests receive inventory, and all later requests are declined once capacity is exhausted.

Specifically, this baseline produces measurements of:

- How total revenue is determined when arrival order, rather than willingness-to-pay, governs allocation.
- How fulfillment distributes across fan archetypes and WTP segments when no allocation logic is applied.
- What proportion of high-WTP demand goes unfulfilled, and what proportion of low-WTP demand is fulfilled, as a consequence of arrival-order assignment.
- The magnitude and structure of unmet demand after the onsale concludes.

### 1.2 Why It Is Included as a Control Condition

The project charter (see `claude.md`, §8) defines success partly as demonstrating superior outcomes relative to FCFS in simulation. To make that comparison rigorous, the FCFS baseline must be executed under identical demand conditions — the same synthetic population, the same inventory, the same event parameters — so that any differences in outcome are attributable solely to the allocation mechanism, not to differences in input.

The FCFS model also provides a concrete reference for the failure modes identified in Phase 0: misallocation of scarce inventory, race-condition dynamics, poor predictability for rights owners, and perceived unfairness for fans. The baseline quantifies the **magnitude** of these failure modes under the Phase 1A demand model so that subsequent approaches can be evaluated against observed (not hypothetical) deficiencies.

## 2. Arrival Time Modeling

### 2.1 Assigning Synthetic Arrival Times

Each fan agent generated by the Phase 1A demand model is assigned a synthetic arrival time representing the moment their purchase request enters the queue. Arrival times are drawn from a distribution that reflects realistic onsale dynamics:

- **Distribution shape.** Arrival times are sampled from a log-normal distribution over the onsale window. This produces a sharp initial surge (the "onsale rush") followed by a long, thinning tail. The majority of the synthetic population arrives within the first minutes; a diminishing fraction trickles in over the remaining window.
- **Onsale window.** The simulation defines a fixed onsale window `[t_0, t_end]`. All arrival times fall within this interval. The window length is a configurable parameter (default: 60 minutes), though the effective allocation period is typically much shorter because inventory exhausts early.
- **Parameterization.** The log-normal parameters (μ_arrival, σ_arrival) are set so that approximately 70% of the population arrives within the first 10% of the onsale window, consistent with observed onsale surge patterns for high-demand events.

### 2.2 Decoupling Arrival Time from Willingness-to-Pay

Arrival time is assigned **independently** of each fan's willingness-to-pay (WTP). There is no correlation — positive or negative — between a fan's WTP and their position in the arrival queue. This is a deliberate modeling choice:

- In a real FCFS onsale, arrival order is determined by factors largely orthogonal to demand intensity: network latency, device speed, queue-management systems, luck, and the use of automated purchasing tools. A fan willing to pay $500 has no structural advantage over a fan willing to pay $50 in securing an early queue position.
- Introducing a correlation between WTP and arrival time would embed an assumption that FCFS implicitly sorts by value, which is precisely the claim being tested. The null assumption — no correlation — is the appropriate baseline.

Arrival time is therefore drawn as an independent random variable for each fan agent, using the same log-normal distribution regardless of archetype or WTP.

### 2.3 Randomness and Batching

- **Random seed.** The arrival time draw uses a fixed random seed so that results are reproducible across simulation runs. When comparing allocation mechanisms, the same seed (and therefore the same arrival sequence) is used to ensure a controlled comparison.
- **No batching.** The FCFS baseline does not batch arrivals. Each fan agent is processed individually in strict arrival-time order. There is no lottery, no windowed grouping, and no randomization of processing order within time windows. This reflects the defining characteristic of FCFS: temporal priority is absolute.

## 3. FCFS Allocation Logic

### 3.1 Step-by-Step Allocation Procedure

The allocation proceeds as follows:

1. **Initialize inventory.** Load the event's tier-level inventory from the Phase 1A event configuration. Each pricing tier has a fixed seat capacity. All seats begin as available.

2. **Sort the request queue.** Order all fan agents by arrival time, ascending. Ties are broken by a secondary random key (assigned at population generation) to avoid deterministic bias among simultaneous arrivals.

3. **Process requests sequentially.** For each fan agent in queue order:
   - a. **Read the request.** The fan's request specifies: preferred pricing tier (or ordered tier preference list), quantity of tickets requested, and a "sit together" flag (boolean).
   - b. **Attempt preferred tier.** Check available inventory in the fan's most-preferred tier.
   - c. **Apply quantity and adjacency constraints** (see §3.2 below).
   - d. **If fulfillable:** Decrement available inventory by the requested quantity. Record the fan as fulfilled at the transacted tier and price. Proceed to the next fan.
   - e. **If not fulfillable in preferred tier:** Attempt the next tier in the fan's preference list, repeating steps (b)–(d).
   - f. **If no tier can fulfill the request:** Record the fan as unfulfilled. Capture the fan's archetype, WTP, and quantity requested for post-simulation analysis. Proceed to the next fan.

4. **Terminate.** Processing continues until all fan agents have been evaluated or all inventory is exhausted. If inventory reaches zero, all remaining fans in the queue are recorded as unfulfilled without further evaluation.

### 3.2 Quantity Requests and "Sit Together" Constraints

- **Quantity.** Each fan requests between 1 and a configurable maximum (default: 6) tickets, drawn from the Phase 1A demand model's group-size distribution. The allocation grants the full requested quantity or nothing — there is no partial fulfillment. If a fan requests 4 tickets and only 3 remain in the tier, the request fails for that tier.
- **Sit together.** When the "sit together" flag is true, the requested tickets must be allocated as a contiguous block. Because the FCFS baseline operates at the tier level (not at seat-map granularity, per `claude.md` §4), this constraint is simplified: the simulation checks only whether the tier has sufficient remaining capacity to satisfy the full quantity. Seat-level adjacency within the tier is not modeled unless explicitly enabled.
- **No splitting across tiers.** A single fan's request is never split across multiple pricing tiers. The fan either receives all requested tickets in one tier or is declined for that tier and falls through to the next preference.

### 3.3 Insufficient Inventory Handling

When a fan's request cannot be satisfied by any tier in their preference list:

- The fan is marked as **unfulfilled**.
- No waitlist, callback, or retry mechanism is modeled. The FCFS baseline is a single-pass system.
- The fan's full record (archetype, WTP, quantity, arrival time) is preserved in the unfulfilled-demand dataset for output analysis.

## 4. Output Metrics

The FCFS baseline produces the following metrics after each simulation run.

### 4.1 Tickets Sold

- **Total tickets allocated** across all tiers.
- **Tickets allocated per tier**, as both absolute count and percentage of tier capacity.
- **Remaining inventory per tier** at simulation end.

### 4.2 Gross Revenue

- **Total gross revenue**: sum of (ticket face price × quantity) for all fulfilled requests.
- **Revenue per tier**: gross revenue disaggregated by pricing tier.
- **Average revenue per ticket**: total gross revenue divided by total tickets sold.
- **Revenue as percentage of theoretical maximum**: gross revenue divided by the revenue that would result if every seat were sold at its tier price (i.e., a sellout at face value). This metric isolates the revenue impact of unfilled high-tier inventory, not WTP-based pricing.

### 4.3 Fulfillment Rate

- **Overall fulfillment rate**: fraction of fan agents who received tickets (fulfilled / total requests).
- **Fulfillment rate by archetype**: for each fan archetype defined in Phase 1A, the fraction of that archetype's members who were fulfilled.
- **Fulfillment rate by requested quantity**: fulfillment rate disaggregated by group size (e.g., fans requesting 1 ticket vs. fans requesting 4).

### 4.4 Distribution of Outcomes by WTP Decile

Fan agents are grouped into deciles by their willingness-to-pay. For each decile, the following are reported:

- **Fulfillment rate**: fraction of fans in the decile who received tickets.
- **Share of tickets received**: proportion of total tickets allocated that went to fans in this decile.
- **Share of revenue contributed**: proportion of total gross revenue attributable to fans in this decile (note: in FCFS at fixed tier pricing, this reflects tier assignment, not WTP directly).
- **Mean arrival time**: average arrival position of fans in the decile, to verify that arrival is not correlated with WTP.

Under the null assumption of independence between WTP and arrival time, fulfillment rates should be approximately uniform across WTP deciles. Deviations from uniformity in a given run reflect sampling variance, not structural bias in the allocation mechanism — though the structural consequence is that FCFS does not *preferentially* fulfill any WTP segment.

### 4.5 Unfulfilled Demand Characteristics

- **Total unfulfilled fan agents**: count and percentage of the population.
- **Total unfulfilled ticket demand**: sum of requested quantities for unfulfilled fans.
- **Unfulfilled demand by archetype**: count and percentage per archetype.
- **Unfulfilled demand by WTP decile**: count and percentage per decile.
- **Aggregate WTP of unfulfilled fans**: the sum (and mean) of WTP across unfulfilled fan agents. This represents the latent economic value that FCFS left unserved — demand that existed but was not reached by the allocation.
- **Quantity-blocked rate**: fraction of unfulfilled fans whose request failed specifically because remaining tier inventory was less than their requested group size (as opposed to zero remaining inventory). This measures the penalty imposed on larger group requests under FCFS depletion dynamics.

## 5. Known Biases and Failure Modes

### 5.1 Structural Biases of FCFS

**FCFS favors arrival speed, not demand intensity or economic value.**

- Fans who arrive earliest receive inventory regardless of their willingness-to-pay, their engagement history, or their group composition. A fan willing to pay the minimum who arrives at t=1 is served before a fan willing to pay a large premium who arrives at t=2.
- Because arrival time is modeled as independent of WTP (§2.2), FCFS allocates inventory in a manner that is **WTP-blind**. The expected share of tickets received by any WTP decile is proportional to its population share, not its value share. High-WTP fans receive no preferential access.

**FCFS penalizes larger group requests.**

- As inventory depletes, fans requesting multiple tickets (especially with "sit together" requirements) are disproportionately likely to be declined. A fan requesting 1 ticket can be fulfilled as long as a single seat remains; a fan requesting 4 tickets fails once fewer than 4 seats remain. This creates a structural penalty against group purchasers that intensifies as the onsale progresses.
- Archetype distributions that correlate with larger group sizes (e.g., families, friend groups) will exhibit lower fulfillment rates than archetypes with smaller typical requests, even at identical arrival time distributions.

**FCFS produces arbitrary cutoff effects.**

- The boundary between fulfilled and unfulfilled fans is determined by a queue position that is itself a random variable. Two fans with identical WTP, identical archetype, and identical preferences may have opposite outcomes based solely on a difference of seconds in arrival time. This arbitrariness is not a bug in the simulation — it is a defining feature of FCFS that the baseline is designed to expose.

**FCFS provides no information to rights owners before the onsale.**

- Because allocation is determined in real time by arrival order, the rights owner has no advance visibility into demand composition, expected revenue, or fulfillment distribution. The outcome is fully determined by a stochastic process (the arrival sequence) that is not observable or controllable.

### 5.2 Relationship to Phase 0 Failure Modes

The following connections map FCFS structural biases to the failure modes defined in Phase 0:

| Phase 0 Failure Mode | FCFS Manifestation |
|---|---|
| **Misallocation of scarce inventory** | Inventory is allocated without regard to WTP or promoter-defined priorities. High-value tiers may be consumed by low-WTP fans who arrived early, while high-WTP fans who arrived later are shut out entirely. |
| **Race-condition dynamics** | FCFS is, by definition, a race. The simulation models the arrival surge (§2.1) that results from fans competing for temporal priority. Outcomes depend on queue position, which is determined by factors outside the fan's substantive relationship with the event. |
| **Poor predictability for rights owners** | Revenue and fulfillment outcomes are functions of the arrival sequence, which varies stochastically across runs. Rights owners cannot forecast revenue or audience composition with confidence before the onsale. |
| **Perceived unfairness** | Fans who are unable to secure tickets despite high willingness to pay, long-standing fandom, or other engagement factors will perceive the system as arbitrary. FCFS offers no mechanism to account for any demand signal beyond arrival time. |
| **Group-size penalty** | Fans purchasing for groups face structurally lower fulfillment probability as inventory depletes, even when per-capita WTP is high. This disproportionately affects certain archetypes (families, social groups) in a manner unrelated to demand intensity. |

### 5.3 What This Baseline Does Not Claim

This baseline does not claim that FCFS produces "bad" outcomes in a normative sense. It identifies the structural properties of FCFS allocation — WTP-blindness, arrival-order dependence, group-size penalty, stochastic cutoffs, and limited rights-owner visibility — and quantifies their magnitude under the Phase 1A demand model. Whether these properties constitute failures depends on the objective function selected by the rights owner, which is outside the scope of this document.
